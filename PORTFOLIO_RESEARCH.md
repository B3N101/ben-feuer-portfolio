# Portfolio Redesign: Research & Direction

_Compiled 2026-11-24_

## Goal

Make the portfolio as professional as possible and improve the odds of landing a mechanical engineering internship when a recruiter or hiring engineer looks at it.

## What Reviewers Want to See

Advice from [MIT ME CommLab](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/meche/commkit/portfolio/), [BU Engineering](https://www.bu.edu/eng/files/2022/07/Key-Factors-of-Engineering-Portfolios.pdf) and [Duke Pratt](https://pratt.duke.edu/news/standing-out-from-the-crowd/) mostly agrees on these points:

1. **The first screen should show who you are, what you want and how to reach you.** Name, "ME @ UMich, seeking Summer 2027 internships," a résumé link and an email.
2. **Each project should state your role and the problem, then show the design → build → test steps** with real numbers and photos.
3. **Make it quick to skim.** A recruiter may spend 30–60 seconds on the site.
4. **Sort projects by skill or topic** so different readers can find what's relevant to them.
5. **Check what you're allowed to share** before posting anything from an internship; some of it may be confidential or company IP.

## How the Current Site Compares

**What works:**
- A dark, full-screen scrolling experience that looks polished.
- Exploded 3D CAD models, a real differentiator that most ME portfolios don't have.
- A spec and materials breakdown for each project.

**What hurts it for hiring:**
- The animated intro and forced snap-scrolling make visitors wait before they see anything.
- Only two projects (Centurion, FTC 24173), and no room in the layout to add more.
- No experience or internship timeline, coursework, or skills section.
- No design → build → test write-up for each project.

## Recommended Direction

Use the **Thanh Tran layout** (A) as the base and keep the **3D model viewer** as the centerpiece of each project page.

- **Visual style:** start with a light or neutral professional look (still to be decided: keep the dark look or go light).
- **Homepage:**
  1. Hero: name, school, internship target, résumé button, email.
  2. Experience / Work timeline.
  3. Project grid with tags (Competition / Team / Personal / Coursework) that can grow past two projects.
  4. Skills and tools (Onshape, Fusion 360, machining, FDM printing, etc.).
  5. Contact footer.
- **Project pages** (one per project):
  - Problem / goal
  - Your role
  - Design steps (sketches, CAD, 3D viewer)
  - Fabrication
  - Testing and results (specs, numbers, placements)
  - What you'd do differently
